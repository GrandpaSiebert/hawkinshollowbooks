# What Families Taught Us

A living memory of what families showed us during the Open the Gate learning phase.

This is not a governance document and not a feature request backlog.

This notebook does not record what we built. It records what families showed us.

We are not listening for votes. We are listening for patterns. We are not trying to make every family happy. We are trying to understand what many families repeatedly teach us about Hawkins Hollow.

Standing reminder: Protect the observations more carefully than the opinions. The observations are the inheritance; the opinions are temporary.

Purpose:

- Capture what families actually experienced.
- Preserve recurring observations until patterns are clear.
- Protect Hawkins Hollow's hospitality voice by learning from real visits.

Working promise:

- Never change the site because of one session. Change it because of a pattern.
- Our responsibility is not to prove our ideas were right. It is to notice when families show us something better.

## How To Use This Notebook

- Run one wander session at a time.
- Record what happened before explaining why it happened.
- Use short, plain notes.
- For the first 10 sessions, keep notes in chronological order.
- After 10 sessions, also update the recurring-pattern sections.
- Prioritize pattern frequency and consistency over isolated anecdotes.
- Prefer observation wording over interpretation wording.
- Example to avoid: "Visitors didn't understand X."
- Example to prefer: "Three visitors paused at X before choosing Y."
- Keep this notebook observation-only. Do not turn entries into feature requests here.
- Move product decisions into a separate planning process after recurring patterns are clear.

## Evidence Loop Rhythm

- Observe 10 family wander sessions.
- Record only what happened.
- Look for recurring patterns.
- Decide whether any pattern is strong enough to justify a change.
- Make the smallest change that addresses that pattern.
- Observe another 10 sessions.

Treat this milestone like a fixed study while it is in progress.

- Do not revise the observation model mid-run unless a genuine collection flaw is discovered.

Success signal:

- After ten real family sessions, we can identify at least one trustworthy recurring pattern strong enough to justify one small, evidence-backed improvement.

At the end of the first ten sessions, answer only these questions:

- What surprised us?
- What kept showing up?
- What assumption did families overturn?
- What single, smallest change deserves to be made before the next ten sessions?

## Learning Review After Session Ten

Schedule a learning review before any feature-planning discussion.

Agenda:

1. What did families consistently teach us?
2. What surprised us?
3. Which assumption held up?
4. Which assumption did not hold up?
5. What improvement has the evidence earned, and what is the smallest change that expresses it?
6. What will we intentionally not change yet because evidence is still weak?
7. If someone who had never met us read only this notebook, what would they learn about the families who visited Hawkins Hollow?

## Stability Window

For the first 10 family sessions:

- Do not edit this framework unless a genuine flaw in the observation process is discovered.
- Focus on collecting honest observations, not improving the wording of the process.

Framework moratorium:

- Do not add new checklist sections, governance documents, or process layers until First Ten Families is complete.
